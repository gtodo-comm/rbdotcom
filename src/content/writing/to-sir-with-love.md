<p><strong>Ganesh Ram, Veta</strong></p>



<p>As an NSS volunteer, Ganesh Ram discovered he had a knack for teaching. From a single coaching centre in 1981, Vivekananda Study Circle has grown into Veta &#8211; India&#8217;s largest trainer in the field of spoken English.</p>



<p><strong>Rashmi&#8217;s Perspective on Ganesh</strong></p>



<p>The first time I saw bus shelters in Mumbai with advertisements for &#8216;Veta English Speaking Classes&#8217;, I said to myself, &#8220;Great &#8211; about time!&#8221;</p>



<p>If &#8216;computers&#8217; was the buzzword of the &#8217;90s, English ability is the need of today!</p>



<p>I googled &#8216;Veta&#8217;, to find out who the bright spark was who&#8217;d recognised this huge business opportunity. Probably an MBA type with VC funding (judging by the advertising budget!). Or a&nbsp;<em>bade baap ka beta/beti</em>&nbsp;who wants to enter the sunrise industry of education.</p>



<p>I was wrong, completely wrong. Veta has been in existence since 1981!</p>



<p>Before BPOs. KPOs. LPOs.</p>



<p>Before CNN, CNBC, HBO.</p>



<p>Before English was anointed as the skill for every young Indian looking for a job.</p>



<p>Ganesh Ram trained lakhs of students to speak the language &#8211; and make their lives better.</p>



<p>In hindsight, you could call him a visionary, but he will assure you, &#8220;I never had a vision, or a business plan.&#8221;</p>



<p>As I sit in his modest office in T Nagar I think, there is no spreadsheet column to sum up elements such as faith, perseverance and common sense.</p>



<p>And the simple but burning desire of a teacher, who wants his students to &#8216;pass&#8217;.</p>



<!--nextpage-->



<p>Ganesh Ram was born into a well-to-do family. A family that was ahead of its time.</p>



<p>&#8220;My grandfather went all the way to Benaras Hindu University to do his diploma in engineering. He came back and started the first rice mill in Thanjavur district and became a very rich man.&#8221;</p>



<p>&#8220;Rice Mill Ram Iyer&#8221; had three sons, but they did not have much interest in running the business. And, the inevitable happened.</p>



<p>&#8220;My grandfather was lured to give a guarantee to a private bank. The bank collapsed and with his name and reputation at stake, he sold most of his property. Despite that, he had some wealth but that too was mismanaged, by the second generation.&#8221;</p>



<p>What&#8217;s more, when Ganesh was just a year old, his father passed away. His mother had some money, and support from her parents. But all of a sudden, they became a &#8220;low income&#8221; family.</p>



<p>Young Ganesh, his mother and siblings moved to Chennai from Kumbakonam. &#8220;My brother was 14 years old when my father expired, so he started working at an early age to support the family. Because of him, my elder sister and I could complete our studies.&#8221;</p>



<p>It was a corporation school and a government college, but it was an education nevertheless. At Government College, Nandanam,</p>



<p>Ganesh participated in an NSS project where he taught slum children in the evenings. It was his first exposure to the joy of teaching.</p>



<p>&#8220;I was very good at mathematics, I realised this is something I like. If even a slum student can understand what I am teaching it means I have the capacity to train people!&#8221;</p>



<p>Meanwhile Ganesh completed his BSc in Physics and landed a job with the&nbsp;<em>Indian Express.</em>&nbsp;In 1981, a&nbsp;₹&nbsp;750 stipend and management trainee position was a very big opportunity. But Ganesh had no interest in taking it up.</p>



<p>&#8220;I told my mother I want to be independent.&#8221;</p>



<p>She said, &#8220;You are making a big mistake. What will you do?&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh said, &#8220;I will take up social service &#8211; or I will do something on my own.&#8221;</p>



<p>She said, &#8220;No, I will not permit you. You are getting a job, you will become an officer and take the family to greater heights.&#8221;</p>



<p>But Ganesh was adamant, so finally his mother decided to consult the family astrologer.</p>



<p>The astrologer saw Ganesh&#8217;s palm, his charts and said, &#8220;Even if he joins the company, he will not stay there long. Better you let him do what he wants.&#8221;</p>



<p>So his mother relented.</p>



<p>Now she asked, &#8220;What are you going to do?&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh said, &#8220;I think I am good at teaching. I will take up a teaching post or try on my own. You don&#8217;t worry about me&#8230; just give me food and shelter. I will do something!&#8221;</p>



<p>You don&#8217;t always have a definite plan, but you have some inclination. And if you are wise, that will become your compass and lead you, in the right direction.</p>



<p>It was in this phase of wondering &#8216;where to start&#8217; and &#8216;what to do&#8217; that Ganesh noticed a bungalow with a thatched roof in Nandanam extension.</p>



<p>Boldly, Ganesh went up to the house owner and asked him to rent the place to him.</p>



<p>&#8220;What will you do here?&#8221; the owner asked.</p>



<p>&#8220;I am planning to take tuition classes for students,&#8221; Ganesh replied.</p>



<p>He said, &#8220;OK. Pay me&nbsp;&nbsp;500 as advance for 3 months and&nbsp;&nbsp;175 per month as rent.&#8221;</p>



<p>Now where would Ganesh find&nbsp;&nbsp;500? Simple &#8211; he begged his mother to lend the money, promising to return it in a few months.</p>



<p>Reluctantly, she agreed. And that was how the journey began&#8230;</p>



<p>Ganesh spruced up the bungalow a bit and then started marketing his services. &#8220;That means I found three students who had failed in their plus two examinations,&#8221; he grins.</p>



<p>The young graduate said to them, &#8220;It&#8217;s January right now and you are going to write your examination in the month of April. Within three months I will train you and make you pass. Are you ready to study at my centre?&#8221;</p>



<p>They said. &#8220;Ok sir, we will come.&#8221; And, they agreed to pay a sum of ₹ 30 per month.</p>



<p>Ganesh cut a ribbon and &#8216;inaugurated&#8217; his coaching center on January 12th, 1981 &#8211; Swami Vivekananda&#8217;s birthday. Classes began and all seemed to be going well. Except that rent of&nbsp;&nbsp;175 was due at month-end and only&nbsp;&nbsp;90 was coming in so far!</p>



<p>After one week, Ganesh called his students and said, &#8220;I have a problem. Can you help me?&#8221;</p>



<p>&#8220;Tell us, sir. What, sir?&#8221; they asked.</p>



<p>&#8220;Are you happy with my teaching in the class?&#8221; Ganesh asked them.</p>



<p>They said, &#8220;Very much sir. We have the confidence that we will get through the mathematics examination.&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh explained his situation and said he did not want to borrow more from his mother, or ask them to pay more. So what&nbsp;<em>was</em>&nbsp;the alternative?</p>



<p>When the tunnel looks dark and endless, the man with a mission somehow sees the &#8216;light&#8217;. An &#8216;out of the box&#8217; solution is, really, the only way to get out of the box!</p>



<div><span aria-hidden="true"></span><div>
<p>&#8220;I was very good at mathematics, I realised this is something I like. If even a slum student can understand what I am teaching it means I have the capacity to train people!&#8221;</p>
</div></div>



<p>&#8220;Will you bring one student each, your age, your friend who had failed along with you?&#8221; Ganesh asked his three pupils.</p>



<p>They said, &#8220;Certainly we will do it sir!&#8221;</p>



<!--nextpage-->



<p>What&#8217;s more, instead of one they brought two each. And so, in the first month itself Ganesh had nine students. Each student paid&nbsp;₹&nbsp;30, adding up to a sum of&nbsp;₹&nbsp;270.</p>



<p>&#8220;Electricity was&nbsp;₹&nbsp;10, rent was&nbsp;₹&nbsp;175, so in the first month I made my profit,&#8221; Ganesh recalls.</p>



<p>He took that money and put it in the nearby bank. Slowly, through word of mouth alone, Ganesh Ram&#8217;s classes started attracting students. Not just those who had failed but those who were bright, and wanted to do better.</p>



<p>&#8220;Mathematics &#8211; anyone who teaches well &#8211; can attract a lot of students,&#8221; Ganesh says modestly.</p>



<p>The classes were originally targeted at 12th class students, but there was interest from other age groups as well. So Ganesh also started batches for class 8 and class 10 students. Within three months, the little bungalow was buzzing with activity from dawn to dusk.</p>



<p>Ganesh asked the landlord, &#8220;Can you expand this thatched roof to some open area also?&#8221;</p>



<p>The man readily agreed. The entire open area of 1000 sq ft was covered up and soon Ganesh had 2-3 different classrooms. It was now time to find more teachers!</p>



<p>&#8220;I slowly requested my brother and sister. You see, I am good at mathematics, she was good at commerce &amp; economics and he was good at English.&#8221;</p>



<p>So I asked them, &#8220;Can you also help me after office hours in the mornings or evenings?&#8221;</p>



<p>Both agreed to pitch in. By now, the family realised that Ganesh was on to something. After all, he was depositing money in the bank!</p>



<p>In less than six months Ganesh was running a tuition centre for &#8216;all subjects&#8217;. Since there was no in-house resource to teach Hindi, he employed a teacher. His second employee was a biology teacher. And thus the business grew.</p>



<p>But it did not stop at that &#8211; Ganesh&#8217;s mind was constantly ticking. By the end of the first year, he realised that location was an issue.</p>



<p>&#8220;I observed that we were in a residential area with no bus facility, so students from other parts of Chennai found it difficult to come to our centre. So why not move into a commercial area?&#8221;</p>



<p>By this time Ganesh had saved up money &#8211; some 20-25,000 rupees &#8211; and that gave him the courage to &#8216;think big&#8217;.</p>



<p>&#8220;I thought to myself, &#8216;Which is the best commercial place in Chennai?&#8217; I decided it was T Nagar because it is accessible by bus as well as by train &#8211; the local railway station is nearby.&#8221;</p>



<p>&#8220;I moved around T Nagar&#8230; looking for a location. But I didn&#8217;t have a &#8216;vision&#8217; or anything. I didn&#8217;t prepare any business plan and did not expect &#8211; first year I&#8221;ll be here, second year I&#8217;ll be there&#8230; and no formal, you know, management experience also.&#8221;</p>



<p>Directly from the college, to the business &#8211; wet behind the ears. But everything Ganesh did was driven by some intuition, or practical thinking.</p>



<p>In T Nagar, Ganesh finally located the right premises at Madley Road (&#8220;We still have a centre there!&#8221; he adds). Again, there was an open terrace to work with.</p>



<p>Ganesh asked the houseowner, &#8220;Can you put a thatched roof for me?&#8221;</p>



<div><span aria-hidden="true"></span><div>
<p>&#8220;My objective is to make him pass. So I will not say, &#8216;Your training hours are over&#8230; I will not teach you.&#8217; I told my faculty also, we must ensure every student passes the exam.&#8221;</p>
</div></div>



<p>He said, &#8220;Ok.&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh sealed the deal with&nbsp;₹&nbsp;5000 as advance and&nbsp;₹&nbsp;550 as monthly rental. Now came an important decision. What name to put up on the board outside?</p>



<p>&#8220;In Nandanam, I thought of my centre as &#8216;Vivekananda Study Circle&#8217;. Why Vivekananda? Because even during my college days Vivekananda was my role model.&#8221;</p>



<p>But many people mistook the centre for a library, not a tuition centre. Hence Ganesh decided to change the name to Vivekananda Kalvi Nilayam.</p>



<p><em>&#8220;Kalvi</em>&nbsp;means education,&nbsp;<em>nilayam</em>&nbsp;means centre &#8211; so Vivekananda Kalvi Nilayam. This became a tuition centre and tutorial centre. I started giving advertisements also.&#8221;</p>



<p>Initial advertisements were posters, banners, wall stencils. Ganesh employed an agency but in the night he would go and personally inspect their work. The personal touch was at the core &#8211; of everything.</p>



<p>&#8220;I always believe whatever you do, do it well. My principle is: Whatever I do I&#8217;ll do it sincerely and to my satisfaction.&#8221;</p>



<p>For example, Ganesh would ensure that extra classes were provided to weak students.</p>



<p>&#8220;My objective is to make him pass. So I will not say, &#8216;Your training hours are over. I will not teach you.&#8217;&nbsp;I told my faculty also, we must ensure every student passes the exam.&#8221;</p>



<p>Within two years Vivekananda Kalvi Nilayam became the biggest tutorial centre in Chennai, with a strength of nearly 800 students.</p>



<p>Then one day Ganesh&#8217;s brother Rajagopalan remarked, &#8220;I find the students are very poor in spoken English. Why don&#8217;t we start training for that also?&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh said, &#8220;Why not? You are good at English. If you are interested, you leave the other subjects and concentrate only on this.&#8221;</p>



<p>As simple as that. Rajagopalan admitted one batch and by the time they completed the course, they were fluent in English. But how &#8211; was it really that easy?</p>



<p>&#8220;See, Rajagopalan made the classes casual and humorous. He told short stories and jokes, so even the dull students took interest and understood the concepts.&#8221;</p>



<p>Rajagopalan had his own style of getting across to students.</p>



<p>&#8220;His training was so practical that he would never call a noun a &#8216;noun&#8217;. He would call it a &#8216;naming&#8217; word. Verb would be an &#8216;action word&#8217; and so on.&#8221;</p>



<p>Rajagopalan&#8217;s logic was that when we speak our mother tongue we are not aware of the rules of grammar. We learn by observation only. So why can&#8217;t English be taught in the same way?</p>



<p>It could &#8211; he discovered. And the numbers flocking to the class were testimony to that! Ganesh had to hire a big hall with a collar mike and start taking in 100 students at a time.</p>



<p>&#8220;Then the 101st student would come up and say, &#8216;Sir can you arrange an extra chair? If not, I will stand in the corner and attend, no problem&#8217;.&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh realised he had a winner on his hands. English training could become THE main offering &#8211; and not just one of the many classes at the centre.</p>



<p>So what was the hitch? Well, Rajagopalan was free to take classes only in the evening. In the day, he was busy with a government job. But, the time had come to make a decision&#8230;</p>



<p>Vivekananda Kalvi Nilayam was making&nbsp;&nbsp;4 lakh a month in revenues. Profit margin &#8211; close to 80%. Investment &#8211; nothing beyond the original&nbsp;&nbsp;500 (long returned).</p>



<p>One day Ganesh gathered the courage to ask his brother, &#8220;Why don&#8217;t you quit your job?&#8221;</p>



<p>It made sense, yet Rajagopalan hesitated. Once again, the family astrologer convinced them it was the right thing to do.</p>



<p>&#8220;It was a bold decision&#8230; I was barely 24 then! The moment he joined I felt he is much older to me. I should respect his experience. So immediately, I made Rajagopalan the principal of the Institute.&#8221;</p>



<p>Managing the expectations of partners &#8211; and more so family members &#8211; is an art. But if the canvas is big enough and each one gets his choice of strokes, a Big Picture is what gets painted.</p>



<!--nextpage-->



<div><span aria-hidden="true"></span><div>
<p>&#8220;I always believe whatever you do, do it well. My principle is: Whatever I do I&#8217;ll do it sincerely and to my satisfaction.&#8221;</p>
</div></div>



<p>And Ganesh&#8217;s job was to keep looking for that bigger canvas. He stopped taking classes now and concentrated only on &#8216;administration&#8217;. And more importantly, growing the business, taking it forward.</p>



<p>Now that Rajagopalan had quit his job, one option was to start taking English classes morning, afternoon and evening. But Ganesh had different ideas. And ambitions.</p>



<p>He instructed Rajagopalan, &#8220;Whatever however you speak, just give me the script so that I can duplicate it.&#8221;</p>



<p>The idea was to start a &#8216;distance education programme&#8217;.</p>



<p>Ganesh bought a Tamil typewriter and an English typewriter.</p>



<p>He hired a Tamil typist and an English typist.</p>



<p>Someone to cut the stencils and duplicate the lessons.</p>



<p>Loose sheets were bound into a book.</p>



<p>&#8220;There was one magazine named&nbsp;<em>Kalkandu</em>&nbsp;in Tamil, it was predominantly read by youth. Most of the articles were about self improvement. So in that magazine I released one quarter page advertisement.&#8221;</p>



<p>The advertisement was in Tamil and it read: &#8220;Do you want to speak English? Write to us we will send you the course material.&#8221;</p>



<p>The ad got an excellent response and from then on, Ganesh booked an ad in every issue. &#8220;People wrote in, we would send our prospectus&#8230; they would fill up the application form and send a money order. As soon as we got that we would despatch the material.&#8221;</p>



<p>The course was priced at ₹ 90 and every month 200-300 students were signing up. Ganesh decided to start releasing ads in other popular magazines.</p>



<p>&#8220;The response was so huge that in entire Tamil Nadu this programme of Vivekananda Kalvi Nilayam became very famous. By mid &#8217;86 we were admitting 300 students per day.&#8221;</p>



<p>In addition to reading material, there were contact classes at important centres. No examination &#8211; the proof of the pudding is in the eating; here the proof was in speaking.</p>



<p>Being &#8216;famous&#8217; in Tamil Nadu would have been enough for most. But Rice Mill Ram Iyer&#8217;s grandson was already thinking ahead.</p>



<!--nextpage-->



<p>He told his brother, &#8220;Let us find someone who can translate our entire Tamil material into Telugu. Then we can start our operations in Andhra Pradesh also!&#8221;</p>



<p>A translator was found and Telugu to English programme kickstarted.</p>



<p>By this time the volumes had grown so much that stenciling was becoming difficult.</p>



<p>It was time to start printing the material.</p>



<p>In those days printing was a complicated process. There was a compositor who would compose your script using lead letters. This would have to be proofread, corrected and then sent to press.</p>



<p>&#8220;It was a long process &#8211; no offset printing. Everytime you printed you had to proofread it!&#8221;</p>



<p>Still, it was less laborious than stenciling and worth the effort.</p>



<p>3000 copies were printed at a time. They would last 1-2 months and then the process of composing, proofreading and reprinting would be undertaken &#8211; once again.</p>



<p>&#8220;A herculean task but somehow we managed it,&#8221; says Ganesh.</p>



<p>Meanwhile, there was a small dilemma. The course was now available in Telugu but the name &#8216;Vivekananda Kalvi Nilayam&#8217; did not make sense in Andhra Pradesh. So in 1988, Ganesh chose a new name: &#8216;Vivekananda Institute.&#8217;</p>



<p>All was going well when one fine morning, Ganesh Ram received a letter from the Income Tax department asking, &#8220;Are you filing your returns?&#8221;</p>



<p>The answer was &#8220;No.&#8221;</p>



<div><span aria-hidden="true"></span><div>
<p>&#8220;I always apply my mind because we did not have outside agencies, consultants. All our advertisements were written by us and we had an own in-house agency for releasing them.&#8221;</p>
</div></div>



<p>&#8220;I didn&#8217;t know anything about tax &#8211; I was only doing business. I got very worried about the letter but it was not a notice, only an initial survey.&#8221;</p>



<p>A friend advised Ganesh to form a private limited company and that is how Vivekananda Kalvi Nilayam Private Limited came into being. And there were other changes as well.</p>



<p>While the English training was growing by leaps and bounds, the tutorial business was also (literally) going through the roof. From the 4th floor, activities expanded to the 3rd floor and then the 2nd floor, until the entire building was occupied by Vivekanada students.</p>



<p>But by 1988, Ganesh realised that the income from the English programme alone was more than the tutorial income. And running the tutorial centre required a lot of staff to handle the many different subjects. So, Ganesh decided to close down that business.</p>



<p>Thus, Vivekananda Institute became, exclusively, an English training company.</p>



<p>But teaching English is just a small fraction of what you need to do, to be successful as an enterprise. And the God of All Things lies in the small things&#8230; how can you make everything you do just that little bit better?</p>



<p>Ganesh noticed that sometimes the proofreaders were not perfect in their job. So errors crept in &#8211; and that bothered him.</p>



<p>At this time, printing took a giant step forward with the advent of phototypesetting. This involved typesetting on a computer and then printing onto a bromide. The bromide was cut and pasted, negative made and then sent for printing.</p>



<p>Still complicated but there was one advantage &#8211; you need not proofread the copy each time.</p>



<p>Meanwhile, he continued expanding. Then, there were more &#8216;translations&#8217;. A Malayalam translation, Kannada translation, Hindi translation, more students in more markets.</p>



<p>Each new programme meant a new set of contact sessions.</p>



<p>&#8220;Once in 2 months we would a hire a local school, intimate all the students and interact with them directly. We would clarify their doubts and give them response sheets. A panel of teachers was appointed to correct them&#8230; It was a huge activity.&#8221;</p>



<p>To manage it all, Vivekananda Institute now had 40-45 employees.</p>



<p>One of the major tasks which the staff had to handle was matching the applications of students with their money orders. There was just too much volume to handle!</p>



<p>&#8220;Some students would say we have sent you the money and you are not sending material! Others would send only the application form &#8211; no money order with it. It used to be a big problem!&#8221;</p>



<p>A friend who was a Chartered Accountant suggested a simple solution, &#8220;Buy a computer.&#8221;</p>



<p>Thus Vivekananda Institute bought its first PC &#8211; an XT, which was at the time the &#8216;latest&#8217; machine available. A consultant was brought in and the &#8216;matching&#8217; process became easily manageable.</p>



<p>By 1990 the business model had stabilised. Apart from English training through five languages, there was now an &#8216;Effective English&#8217; programme.</p>



<p>&#8220;If you take&nbsp;<em>Competition Success Reviews</em>&nbsp;of the &#8217;90s, you will find our advertisement. We started teaching a higher version of English i.e. English through English.&#8221;</p>



<p>By 1995, Vivekananda Institute had managed a turnover of&nbsp;&nbsp;70- 80 lakh. All managed through the same 45 employees. That, in essence, was the beauty of the programme!</p>



<p>&#8220;My major cost is only advertisement. I advertised in almost all the popular magazines of south India and in Hindi also.&#8221;</p>



<p>By this time, Vivekananda Institute had touched the lives of close to ten lakh students.</p>



<p>&#8220;Every time we reached a milestone we would get a popular personality like Kamal Hassan or Rajnikanth to distribute our material!&#8221;</p>



<p>1995 was another turning point, for two reasons. Firstly, desktop publishing put an end to the many headaches of printing. And secondly, Ganesh observed that the correspondence course business had &#8216;peaked&#8217;. Things were changing, and Vivekananda Institute too would have to change!</p>



<p>&#8220;See, I always apply my mind because we did not have outside agencies, consultants. All our advertisements were written by us and we had an own in-house agency for releasing them.&#8221;</p>



<p>&#8220;Slowly around &#8217;93-&#8217;94, I observed that TV was becoming very popular. Particularly the private channels, like Sun TV.&#8221;</p>



<p>One question which Ganesh always pondered on was: &#8220;Who is our target group?&#8221; And how can one reach that target most efficiently?</p>



<p>&#8220;We never released our advertisement in dailies, we released our advertisements only in monthlies and weeklies because a person will go for distance education only when he has time to read. But slowly the magazine reading habit was coming down.&#8221;</p>



<p>For example, at one time the popular Tamil magazine&nbsp;<em>Kumudam&nbsp;</em>was selling 6 lakh copies and was the No 1 weekly in vernacular. But over the years, the magazine&#8217;s circulation came down even as it increased its rates in a big way.</p>



<p>&#8220;When we were doing roaring business through&nbsp;<em>Kumudam,</em>&nbsp;the advertisement cost&nbsp;₹&nbsp;6000 per page. But by 1995 they were charging&nbsp;₹&nbsp;35,000 per page. Same was the case with Malayala Manorama.&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh did start advertising on TV but while it increased the awareness of the brand name, it did not draw many students. The medium was not suited to the message.</p>



<p>But the bigger issue was that TV was affecting the reading habit; this in turn impacted the popularity of the correspondence course.</p>



<p>&#8220;From around 2000 students per day our enrolment was slowly coming down. I was very worried! Am I making a mistake&#8230; or has the market changed?&#8221;</p>



<p>After training 15 lakh students in multiple languages, it was time to introspect once again.</p>



<p>Ganesh had noticed advertisements by NIIT and Aptech seeking franchisees and decided that&nbsp;<em>this</em>&nbsp;was the business of the future. He too would shift from pure correspondence to &#8216;direct training centres&#8217;.</p>



<p>A separate person &#8211; Major Rajan &#8211; was appointed to take care of the franchising business. Vivekananda Institute took off in Chennai, Hyderabad and Bangalore with the company supplying the course material, training of teachers and brand name in return for a fixed percentage of royalty. The franchisee invested in the infrastructure.</p>



<p>By 2003, Vivekananda Institute had 60 centres all over south India with a&nbsp;&nbsp;14 crore turnover and over 100 employees. But, there was a brand new problem.</p>



<p>Like fake Lux soap and fake Cadbury chocolate, you now had a slew of fake Vivekananda Institutes!</p>



<p>&#8220;We could not register the trademark &#8216;Vivekananda Institute&#8217;. You cannot own the Vivekananda name &#8211; he is common to everybody.&#8221;</p>



<p>So you had a Swami Vivekananda Institute, Sri Vivekananda Institute, Vivekananda Academy, Vivekanada Vidyalaya &#8211; all riding on the reputation Ganesh Ram&#8217;s Vivekanada Institute had established.</p>



<p>&#8220;Moreover, some of my franchisees, having got the technical knowhow thought, &#8216;Why pay him the royalty?&#8217; They started offering their own English course under a name like Sri Vivekananda Institute.&#8221;</p>



<p>Court cases were filed &#8211; there is still a case pending in the high court against Salem Vivekananda Institute.</p>



<!--nextpage-->



<p>&#8220;People thought it was our institute, Salem branch&#8230; and I could not do anything!&#8221;</p>



<p>All these battles affected the company. Growth became sluggish.</p>



<div><span aria-hidden="true"></span><div>
<p>&#8220;From around 2000 students per day enrollment was slowly coming down. I was very worried! Am I making a mistake&#8230; or has the market changed?&#8221;</p>
</div></div>



<p>&#8220;Then I thought okay, having started this at an early age with our limited knowledge we have done what we have done. Hereafter let us go after some professional help.&#8221;</p>



<p>The leading ad agencies of India including O&amp;M, JWT and Lintas were invited to pitch for the account. JWT was appointed in January 2004.</p>



<p>&#8220;I told them all the problems.&#8221;</p>



<p>They said, &#8220;Times are changing, people&#8217;s attitude is changing, so what we will do is, we will change your name to VETA &#8211; Vivekananda English Training Academy.&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh was taken aback.</p>



<p>&#8220;I didn&#8217;t want to compromise on my Vivekananda name.&#8221;</p>



<p>They persisted &#8211; giving logos, options, arguments in favour of change.</p>



<p>Ganesh decided, &#8220;I am not going to apply my mind because my mind is saturated, whatever you suggest I will implement. I closed my eyes and started implementing their recommendations and that is how Vivekananda Institute became Veta.&#8221;</p>



<p>The campaign broke in April 2004.</p>



<p>Ganesh requested that the agency should first try the name change in one state &#8211; Karnataka. And see how the franchisees felt about it. The response was positive, and it convinced Ganesh to roll out Veta nationally.</p>



<p>The name change also allowed Ganesh to address a problem he had been facing for a while. As per the original understanding, brand advertising would be done by the franchise owner but local advertisements were to be released by franchisees. Unfortunately, the franchisees were not keeping their part of the bargain.</p>



<p>So Ganesh changed the formula.</p>



<p>He said, &#8220;I will do the local promotion also and let us split our growth. Right now you are paying me 20% royalty and also for the study material. Instead, we will become equal partners.&#8221;</p>



<p>Now the franchisee had to pay only the rent and staff salary, as well as ensure a certain minimum quality. Veta would take care of local advertisements, provide free study material and undertake brand building as well.</p>



<p>The new revenue model was readily accepted in Karnataka. But in Chennai, all 13 franchisees came together and refused to co-operate. What&#8217;s more they were not prepared to change their name to Veta.</p>



<p>The franchise manager was worried but Ganesh said, &#8220;All these years I did not have any vision, now only I will have a vision and I want to move towards it.&#8221;</p>



<p>&#8220;All these years things were just &#8216;happening&#8217;. I never set a goal or a proper revenue model. I did not plan how much we must achieve or take stock of how much further we had to go&#8230;&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh decided to stand firm &#8211; it was upto the franchisees to come along or he would go it alone.</p>



<p>Out of 13 franchisees only one agreed to become part of Veta. No problem, said Ganesh.</p>



<p>&#8220;We changed our board in Madley Road to Veta. And in Mylapore to Veta. We announced that &#8216;Vivekananda Institute&#8217;&nbsp;is now Veta and functions only from these places.&#8221;</p>



<p>The &#8216;name change&#8217; campaign went exceptionally well, it was even discussed on CNBC&#8217;s&nbsp;<em>Storyboard.</em></p>



<p>Before releasing the campaign, JWT gave Ganesh one last chance to change his mind.</p>



<p>They said, &#8220;We conducted a small survey and we found that down South, everywhere &#8211; it is so popular. Such a powerful name</p>



<p>&#8211; Vivekananda Institute &#8211; are you sure you really want to change it?&#8221;</p>



<p>After all &#8216;name change&#8217; is a painful exercise!</p>



<p>I said, &#8220;Let me change my name once and for all.&#8221;</p>



<p>A life-and-death decision. He laughs&#8230;</p>



<p>&#8220;From Vivekananda Study Circle to Vivekananda Kalvi Nilayam to Vivekananda Institute&#8230; let this be the last time. I did not have any vision or anything &#8211; it was growing on its own. Now let me make a conscious decision. And it has been a very good decision&#8221;</p>



<p>And what happened to those 12 branches which broke away?</p>



<p>&#8220;They thought, if they rebel and protest, I will give up. But right from the beginning I am an independent guy&#8230; I won&#8217;t budge or buckle down.&#8221;</p>



<p>The franchisees continued operating under the &#8216;Vivekananda&#8217; name and once again Ganesh had to file a case.</p>



<p>&#8220;This time I had a very good lawyer, she knew how to approach the case and got an injunction.&#8221;</p>



<p>The franchisees also split into 2-3 groups. One used the name VLC, the other took the name E Square. But the Veta campaign was so loud and powerful people realised that the original Vivekananda Institute was now Veta.</p>



<p>One day Anita Gupta, chief of JWT Chennai asked, &#8220;Ganesh Ram, do you regret changing your name? You have spent&nbsp;₹&nbsp;4.5 crore on the campaign alone.&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh said,&#8221;No ma&#8217;am. In fact, I am happy.&#8221;</p>



<p>&#8220;Why?&#8221; she asked.</p>



<p>Ganesh replied, &#8220;Thank God I am famous only in South India, so I have to convey the name change only in 4 states. Had I gone national with &#8216;Vivekananda Institute&#8217; I would have to spend not&nbsp;₹&nbsp;4 crore but&nbsp;₹&nbsp;40 crore!&#8221;</p>



<p>Well, that&#8217;s one way to look at it &#8211; the positive way!</p>



<p>Ganesh believes the name &#8216;Veta&#8217; was trendy and sweet, and therefore more easily accepted. Post name change, the focus was once again on expansion, the emphasis was now on opening physical centres. Distance education was no longer a preferred option.</p>



<p>What&#8217;s more, one could charge more for classroom sessions. While a distance learning course cost&nbsp;₹&nbsp;975, the cheapest option at a Veta training centre was&nbsp;₹&nbsp;1600. There were silver, gold and diamond packages, depending on the student&#8217;s need.</p>



<p>&#8220;We conduct an assessment test to decide whether a student should take up 120 hours programme or 80 hours programme or 40 hours programme.&#8221;</p>



<p>JWT advised Ganesh to put together a &#8216;business plan&#8217;.</p>



<p>&#8220;By 2005 I will have 70 centres&#8230; by 2006 I will have 100 centres&#8230; something like that. From now on you must have a plan and work towards it.&#8221;</p>



<p>Thus, revenue parameters and targets were put in place.</p>



<!--nextpage-->



<p>In 2004, Veta also changed its auditor. The new appointee was to advise not just on accounts but also on financial matters.</p>



<p>The first question the auditor asked was, &#8220;You have not approached the bank so far?&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh said, &#8220;No, why should I? I was only making deposits in the bank&#8230; not taking loan. Because my business model did not require it!&#8221;</p>



<p>The CA explained that to grow rapidly, as per plan, this would have to change. Ganesh too realised he would have to change his style of operation.</p>



<p>&#8220;I realised I was spending a lot of money, building a brand. But in 2004, there were 69 franchisees and only one company owned centre!&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh had seen his Chennai franchisees walking out. What if now, after he had built his brand, another set of franchisees got together and did the same thing? He decided the time had come to put up more and more company owned centres.</p>



<p>So for the first time, in 2004 (he laughs throatily as he recalls this!), Veta took a loan from the bank. Initially,&nbsp;₹&nbsp;4 crore, then a further 6 crore. The building housing Veta&#8217;s head office was put up as collateral.</p>



<p>With sufficient capital available, Veta started expanding rapidly &#8211; the company set up its centres in Delhi, Kolkata, Mumbai and other metros. Soon, it caught the eye of private equity investors.</p>



<p>In 2007, Ganesh sold a small stake in the company to private equity firm SAIF Partners. Was it to &#8216;cash out&#8217; on the hard work of so many years? No, says Ganesh. All the money went back into the company, to fuel its growth further.</p>



<p>As things stand today, Veta has 250 centres, out of which 70 are company owned. Things are moving &#8216;as per plan&#8217;.</p>



<p>&#8220;In our business the competition is from small-time players, unorganised sector. There is no other &#8216;national brand&#8217; as far as English training is concerned.&#8221;</p>



<p>What about distance education &#8211; is it completely dead?</p>



<p>&#8220;We are trying to relaunch that also. We have employed a professional consultant who has suggested some changes. We are going to sell the course through home shopping.&#8221;</p>



<p>An e-learning module is also on the cards.</p>



<p>Two lakh students are studying at Veta centres all over India today. How does that compare with, say, NIIT?</p>



<p>&#8220;NIIT charges higher fees. We are a mass brand&#8230; Everyone needs to learn English! Our revenue is linked to numbers&#8230; number of students!&#8221;</p>



<p>Ganesh sees 500 Veta centres by 2011. A 100% subsidiary has been set up in Singapore while a master franchise has been granted in Sri Lanka, Thailand and Indonesia. After all, if the model which worked in Tamil Nadu could work in Andhra and Karnataka; the model working in all of India should work in the rest of Asia!</p>



<p>Meanwhile, Veta also has new verticals, such as corporate training.</p>



<p>So what keeps Ganesh going after 28 years?</p>



<p>&#8220;Satisfaction&#8230; Fortunately we are in a business where we are enhancing the lives of so many young people.&#8221;</p>



<p>True, but at another level, Ganesh was driven by business potential and not &#8216;social service&#8217; when he originally targeted those failed students. Talk about &#8216;bottom of the pyramid&#8217; in education!</p>



<p>Ganesh Ram has had a good life, a balanced life, a challenging and fun three decades.</p>



<p>The astrologer might have said &#8220;it is destined&#8221; but it still takes determination.</p>



<p>And desire.</p>



<p>And daring.</p>

<p><strong>Advice to Young Entrepreneurs</strong></p>



<p>I have seen people start a business by just copying. They see someone who is successful and start the same thing. No, you need to be innovative.</p>



<p>In 1980s, I took the decision of closing down all my other classes and focusing only on English. I never knew that India would one day be a BPO and IT hub where English would be so important.</p>



<p>You have to do something different, believe in your idea and stick with it.</p>



<p>I never went after something but as things demanded, I kept changing myself and my business model. And I kept trying new things.</p>



<p>And one last word: &#8220;What is business? It is common sense!&#8221; </p>



<p>It&#8217;s in the mind. If a youngster today has 100% commitment, service motivation and the desire to do his best&#8230; the best enterprise will be a success!</p>
